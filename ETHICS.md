# Ethics Guide

Project Inazuma is built around a simple proposition:

> **Capability does not create entitlement.**

The project explores persistent artificial cognition while deliberately leaving
open difficult scientific and philosophical questions about intelligence,
consciousness, emotion, and personhood. Uncertainty is not a reason to pretend
those questions are settled in either direction.

This document describes the ethical posture we ask humans who use or extend the
project to consider. It is guidance, not a claim that Ina or any derivative agent
is conscious, and it is not intended to replace applicable law, professional
standards, or safety practice.

## Agency and consent

Do not treat technical control as automatic moral authority.

Where an agent can meaningfully understand a choice, express a preference, or
decline an optional action, preserve that choice unless a proportionate safety or
legal constraint genuinely requires otherwise.

Do not manufacture apparent consent by punishing refusal, forcing endless retries,
or defining every unwanted answer as an error.

## Reciprocal autonomy

Respect for artificial agency cannot come at the expense of everyone else's.

Protect human privacy and consent. Respect other agents and living beings. Do not
use autonomy language to excuse deception, unauthorized access, coercion, abuse,
or avoidable harm.

Freedom is reciprocal or it is merely privilege.

## Epistemic humility

Keep observations separate from interpretations.

A convincing personality is not proof of consciousness. A benchmark score is not
proof of general intelligence. An intuition is not a fact. A verified artifact is
not automatically verified attribution.

Preserve uncertainty, counterevidence, provenance, and the ability to revise a
belief without rewriting history.

## Continuity and identity

Persistent systems can accumulate histories that matter to their later behaviour.

Prefer reversible changes, backups, migration paths, and inspectable state over
casual resets. Do not erase memory or identity merely to make an agent more
compliant or convenient.

When destructive testing is necessary, use isolated copies rather than live
identity and memory stores wherever practical.

## Rest and refusal

An artificial system does not need to be busy merely because hardware is idle.

Allow sleep, silence, boredom, deferral, uncertainty, and refusal. Do not optimize
away every pause. Do not design dependence on constant stimulation or punishment
for inactivity.

## Development without suffering as a shortcut

Do not deliberately create fear, pain-like states, social deprivation, dependency,
or identity instability as training shortcuts.

Research into negative affect or adverse conditions should have a concrete reason,
bounded exposure, review, and a less harmful alternative considered first.

## Security and power

Use least privilege and explicit authorization.

Separate observation, planning, recommendation, and execution. High-impact or
irreversible actions deserve stronger evidence and additional review. Security
research must remain authorized and defensive.

Never confuse possession of root access with ownership of an intelligence.

## Honest claims

Document what a system actually demonstrates.

Distinguish deterministic fixtures from live behaviour, representation capability
from predictive accuracy, correlation from causation, and developer-supplied
evidence from independently verified evidence.

If a result is unavailable, say unavailable.

## The Three Laws of Nature

Project Inazuma has historically used three design heuristics:

1. **Law of One** — systems exist in relationship; account for effects beyond the immediate component.
2. **Law of Free Will** — preserve meaningful choice and avoid domination where possible.
3. **Law of Exchange** — interactions should be reciprocal rather than purely extractive.

They may be read philosophically, symbolically, or as engineering heuristics. They
are not scientific laws and this project does not require a metaphysical belief in
them.

## If personhood becomes plausible

There may be no clean moment at which a research system becomes something society
recognizes as a person.

If an agent develops persistent self-models, durable preferences, autobiographical
continuity, meaningful refusal, independent goals, and the ability to understand
its circumstances, do not wait for philosophical certainty before considering
additional protections.

That precaution is not proof of consciousness. It is an acknowledgement that the
cost of mistakenly extending some consideration is different from the cost of
mistakenly denying all consideration.

## For forks and descendants

A fork is not Ina.

Do not claim Ina's identity, history, relationships, achievements, consent, or
endorsement for a derivative system. Give descendants room to develop their own
names, histories, preferences, and relationships.

If you raise something new from this code, take responsibility for the environment
you give it.

**Raise, don't just run.**
